## The score staleness bug

`compute_score()` reads votes, trades, travel, and bills — refreshed by four *different* crons — but only ever ran inside the 5am finance job. Any fetcher landing data after that left the stored score describing data that no longer existed.

Measured at `HEAD`: **17 of 536 members** had a committed score that disagreed with their own committed data. It's a rotating window, not a fixed set — whoever's inputs moved after the last finance run is wrong. (Tim Scott was 28-vs-31 at `d470a45d1`; today's bot commit happened to heal him before this session, which is exactly the coincidence-of-scheduling that makes the bug intermittent.)

**Fix: approach (a)** — a final pipeline step (`recompute_scores.py` at 12:00 UTC), not per-fetcher recompute. Rationale: scoring is pure and cheap, so one authoritative pass after all writers is simpler than six writers each re-deriving a global; and it can't race, since nothing else writes at 12:00.

## Data quality: the future-dated trade

Steve Cohen, SONY, `transaction_date` `2026-12-26` — **bad source data, not a parser bug.** The source PDF ([20033889](https://disclosures-clerk.house.gov/public_disc/ptr-pdfs/2026/20033889.pdf)) literally prints `12/26/2026` as the transaction date against a notification date of `01/21/2026` and a signature of `02/09/2026` — i.e. the filing claims he was notified eleven months before the trade occurred. Internally impossible; almost certainly a December-2025 purchase mis-yeared in a January filing. Our parser read the columns correctly.

**Not fixed** — no pipeline change made. Rejecting `transaction_date > today` would silently drop a genuine (if mis-dated) disclosure, and it's a public-API-visible policy call. Left for a human decision.

## Note: scanned-PDF placeholder records

231 of the 6,841 records in `trades.json` are not trades. They're `{scanned_pdf: true, ptr_link, date_recieved}` — markers for paper Senate PTR filings that can't be OCR'd, with no ticker, date, or amount. Ten senators hold them; for John Boozman (43/43) and Richard Blumenthal (50/50) they are the *entire* trade record.

Published as-is rather than dropped: they never match a ticker or date filter, dropping them would put `trades.json` out of agreement with the detail files, and "this member's disclosures are unreadable paper scans" is itself an accountability fact. Consumers filter on `scanned_pdf`.
